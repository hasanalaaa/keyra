The press

Nothing types until you press.

Pick an account on your phone and Keyra gets ready. Its light breathes blue, at the same 1.2-second rhythm as the LED on the board, until you press the button. Then it types into the field you clicked, and the line closes.

Malware on your computer can’t press a button.

  • 60 sArmed once. An action waits a minute, then expires. It runs once.
  • 1.5 sLong press to cancel, or to lock the vault when nothing is waiting.

How it works

From plug to typed in four steps.

The computer sees a keyboard typing the one login you chose, at the moment you chose. Your phone is the screen; Keyra is the vault.

  1. 01

    Plug it in

    Connect Keyra’s USB port to the computer. It shows up as a keyboard. Or pair it once over Bluetooth.

  2. 02

    Join its Wi-Fi

    On your phone, join Keyra-XXXX and open keyra.local. Nothing to install, no account.

  3. 03

    Pick an account

    Unlock with your passphrase, tap an account and choose Username, Password, Both or a 2FA code.

  4. 04

    Press the button

    Click the login field, press Keyra’s button, and it types. The light flashes green and your phone says Typed.

Why Keyra

Move the secret off the computer.

Most password managers live in the same computer and browser that attackers target. Keyra keeps the vault somewhere else: in your hand.

  • Your vault never lives on the computer

    Nothing is stored or synced there. The computer only sees a keyboard typing the one login you chose. The optional browser extension only asks Keyra to type; it never holds a password.

  • Malware cannot make it type

    Every action needs a physical press of the button on the device. No press, no typing.

  • No cloud, no account, no subscription

    The vault is encrypted on the device and managed over the device’s own Wi-Fi. Nothing leaves your desk.

  • Works on any computer

    If it accepts a USB keyboard (a work laptop, a locked-down kiosk, a TV), Keyra works there. Phones and tablets pair over Bluetooth.

  • Passkeys and a security key

    Over USB, Keyra is also a FIDO2/U2F security key for up to 50 passkeys. Each sign-in waits for your press. Not FIDO certified.

  • Open, and a few dollars

    MIT-licensed firmware for a stock development board. Read it, build it, change it. The app speaks Arabic and English.

Security model

Honest about what it protects.

Keyra is a convenience-and-isolation device, not a magic shield. Here is exactly what it does, and what it doesn’t.

What Keyra protects

  • Every entry is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a random key, protected by your passphrase (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, about 1.2 s on the device).
  • Unlock attempts are rate-limited and counted durably; restarting doesn’t reset the delay.
  • It types only after a physical press. The computer gets no storage or network channel to Keyra.
  • Passwords reach your phone only after a press (on by default), so a stolen browser session reads nothing.
  • Apps, the extension and AI agents get arm-only tokens: they can ask Keyra to type, never read.
  • Bluetooth pairing opens for 2 minutes, only after a press. The rest of the time only paired devices can connect.
  • Decrypted data lives in RAM only while unlocked, and is wiped on lock. It locks when the computer is unplugged or sleeps.
  • Updates must be signed with your release key, never older, and roll back by themselves if they fail to start.

What it does not protect

  • The phone link is plain HTTP over WPA2, not TLS. Someone who knows Keyra’s Wi-Fi password and is on that network can observe the traffic.
  • No secure element. With the board in hand, an attacker can dump the flash and guess your passphrase offline. Use a long one.
  • Flash encryption and secure boot are optional and off by default. They are irreversible eFuse steps.
  • Not independently audited.
  • A keylogger on the computer can see what Keyra types, as with any keyboard.
  • The security key is not FIDO certified and uses self attestation; its keys are as safe as your passphrase.
  • Bluetooth pairing is “Just Works”. Someone in range during the 2-minute window could pair their own device. Check the device list afterwards.

The full threat model, cryptographic design and rate-limit schedule: SECURITY.md. Found a vulnerability? Please use a private GitHub security advisory.

Printable enclosure

A case you can print.

For the VCC-GND YD-ESP32-S3 board, the dual USB-C DevKitC-1 clone, in two versions: on a cable, or as a dongle that plugs straight into the port. Five printed parts, no screws. A captive mechanical button presses the board’s own BOOT switch, a light ring carries the LED’s glow around it, and the Contour is debossed in the lid.

The cable version has been printed and fitted; the dongle’s USB-C coupler has not been fitted yet. It does not fit Espressif’s own DevKitC-1, so check your board against the measurements first.

Install

Install Keyra from your browser.

Flash the latest release onto a new board in about a minute. No tools to install.

Installing erases the board.

Everything on it is wiped: any Keyra vault, passkeys and settings. Use this for a new board.

Already using Keyra? Don’t install here. It updates itself and keeps your vault: Device → Firmware update.

  1. Choose your boardNot listed? The DevKitC-1 build should run on ESP32-S3 boards with 8 MB flash, a native USB port and a BOOT button.
  2. Put it in download modeUnplug the board. Hold BOOT, plug the board’s USB port into this computer, then let go. On the UART port no button is needed.
  3. InstallTick the box, click Install, choose the board’s serial port and confirm. Keep the cable in until it says done.
  4. Restart and set it upPress RESET, or unplug and plug it back in. When the light breathes amber, join Keyra-XXXX on your phone (password keyra1234) and open http://keyra.local.

Needs Chrome or Edge on a desktop computer (Web Serial) and a USB data cable.

FAQ

Before you start.

More in the README.

Will installing here erase my Keyra?

Yes. The installer is for new boards: it wipes the whole flash, vault included. A Keyra you already use updates from its own app (Device → Firmware update) and keeps your accounts. To move to a new board, make an encrypted backup in the app first, install, then restore it.

Which browsers can install?

Chrome or Edge (and other Chromium browsers with Web Serial) on Windows, macOS or Linux. Firefox, Safari and phones can’t reach USB serial ports; use the esptool command under Install instead.

The installer doesn’t find my board.

Use a USB data cable (some cables only charge), put the board in download mode (hold BOOT, plug in, let go), and close other apps that might hold the port, such as a serial monitor. On Windows the UART port may need the CP210x driver.

Can the computer read my vault?

No. Keyra presents a keyboard and a security key, with no storage interface and no network path. The computer sees what gets typed, and can ask the security key to sign a website’s challenge, which needs your press.

Is Keyra a YubiKey replacement?

No. Keyra can create and use passkeys over USB, and for many personal accounts that works well. A YubiKey keeps its keys in a secure chip, is FIDO certified and works over NFC. Keep a second key or another sign-in method on every account.

Why HTTP and not HTTPS?

A device on its own network can’t get a certificate browsers trust without installing something on each phone, and a browser warning teaches people to ignore warnings. The link is protected by WPA2 and a private Wi-Fi password instead. It’s a real limit, listed above.

Do I need the internet?

No. Keyra makes its own Wi-Fi network. If you let it join your home Wi-Fi, it uses the internet only for the time and to check for firmware updates when you ask.

What does it cost?

A compatible ESP32-S3 board is usually a few dollars to about ten. The firmware is free and MIT licensed.
